Session notes are not a bureaucratic obligation — they are the foundation of intelligent programming. Without them, every conversation about progress is based on memory; with them, you have a reliable record of what was loaded, what was observed, and what comes next.

Good session notes are fast to write (under five minutes after the session), focused on what matters, and stored securely. This template gives you a format you can use immediately.

---

## What Belongs in Session Notes

**Record:**
- Date, session number, and duration
- Exercises, sets, reps, and load (in the unit you actually use — kg or lb, consistent throughout)
- Subjective feel of the session (the client's report, not your interpretation)
- Technical observations — what you noticed about movement quality
- Any modifications made and why
- Next session focus or adjustments

**Do not record:**
- Medical diagnoses or speculative health assessments
- Information that isn't relevant to training delivery
- More personal detail than necessary (the notes are about the session, not the person's life story)

Session notes may be subject to data access requests from clients depending on your jurisdiction. Write accordingly — factual, professional, focused on training.

OBSERVATIONS

## Tracking Progress Accurately

**Use consistent units.** Pick kilograms or pounds for all clients and stick to it. Converting mid-program creates errors. If a client trains in both a home gym and a commercial gym with different equipment, note the equipment or context.

**Record actual completed work, not planned work.** If the plan was 4 sets of 8 at 40 kg but the client completed 3 sets of 8 and one set of 6, record what happened.

**Making progress comparisons.** The template includes session numbers precisely for this purpose. "Session 14 vs. session 11" gives you a concrete reference point. Keep prior sessions accessible — in a folder by client, sorted chronologically.

**Subjective data matters.** An objective increase in load means less if the client reported zero energy and was helped by adrenaline. Context makes the number meaningful.

---

## Secure Data Storage

Session notes contain personal data. At minimum:

- Store files password-protected or in encrypted cloud storage
- Do not leave paper notes unattended in shared spaces
- Have a clear retention policy — how long you keep records and what happens when a client relationship ends
- If you use a practice management app, check its privacy policy and data location

In many jurisdictions these are legal obligations under data protection law — check the specific requirements that apply to your location and business structure.

---

## Common Pitfalls

**Writing notes hours or days later.** Memory degrades fast. Three minutes immediately after a session is far more reliable than 30 minutes the next morning.

**Recording only numbers.** Load and reps without movement quality notes leaves you replicating exactly the same session rather than building on it.

**Inconsistent load units.** Mixing kg and lb in the same client file creates genuine errors when comparing sessions. Pick one.

**Over-documenting client disclosures.** If a client mentions something personal, you don't need to log it in full. "Client reported elevated stress this week" is enough if it affected the session. Sensitive personal disclosures that don't affect programming don't belong in the training log at all.
