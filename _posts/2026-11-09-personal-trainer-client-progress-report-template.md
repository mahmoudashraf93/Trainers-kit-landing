A monthly progress report is not a longer version of the weekly check-in. The check-in is a quick pulse — it captures how the current week felt and flags anything that needs attention before the next session. The monthly report steps back, synthesises trends across four weeks, and creates a natural point for a collaborative conversation about what's working and what should change.

A shared report gives the client a chance to recognize progress, discuss obstacles, and decide what they would like to do next.

---

## Check-In vs. Progress Report: Key Differences

| Feature | Weekly check-in | Monthly progress report |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 5 questions, 2 minutes | One full page, reviewed together |
| Focus | This week's experience | Trends over the past four weeks |
| Initiated by | Trainer sends a prompt | Trainer prepares and shares |
| Action required | Immediate session adjustment | Program direction decision |
| Tone | Informal, ongoing | Reflective, collaborative |
| Shared with client | Yes (it's their response) | Yes — ideally reviewed together |

## Completed Example

**Client:** Sofía, 31, three months into twice-weekly training. Goal: build upper-body strength and improve posture after years of desk work.

---

**MONTHLY PROGRESS REPORT**
Client: Sofía R.  Period: 1 October – 31 October 2026
Trainer: J. Osei  Report #: 3  Sessions this month: 7 of 8 planned

---

**SECTION 1 — GOALS RECAP**

Original goal: Build upper-body strength, particularly pressing and pulling; feel more comfortable during the workday.

Target metrics: Increase seated row load; progress from incline to full push-up; maintain weekly mobility routine.

---

**SECTION 2 — OBJECTIVE PROGRESS**

Seated cable row:
  Start: 32 kg × 3 × 10   End: 40 kg × 3 × 10   Change: +8 kg (+25%)

Push-up variation:
  Start: Incline push-up × 8   End: Full push-up × 5   Change: Progressed to floor

Upper-back mobility (self-report, 1–5 scale):
  Start: 2/5 ("stiff most mornings")   End: 3.5/5 ("noticeably better")   Change: +1.5

Attendance: 7 of 8 sessions (1 missed due to illness — rescheduled)

---

**SECTION 3 — EXPERIENCE AND OBSERVATIONS**

Client-reported highlights: "I sat through a 3-hour meeting last week and didn't have the usual neck ache afterwards — that's never happened before." Reported improved confidence in the gym during solo sessions.

Movement quality: Scapular retraction during row movements has improved substantially. Shoulder girdle stability in overhead reach noticeably better than in month 1. Hip hinge remains a work in progress under load.

Notable challenges: Week 3 was disrupted by a mild illness (one missed session). The client asked for more time to practice the full push-up setup.

---

**SECTION 4 — WHAT'S WORKING**

Sofía increased the load on the seated row under the recorded conditions. The 10-minute morning mobility routine has been completed consistently (reported in check-ins). The functional win with the meeting posture was clearly motivating.

---

**SECTION 5 — WHAT TO ADJUST**

Hip hinge pattern needs attention before increasing deadlift load further. Review the push-up setup together. Consider adding direct thoracic mobility work as a warm-up staple.

---

**SECTION 6 — NEXT PERIOD FOCUS**

Primary focus: Hinge pattern and building to 10 full push-ups.

Behavior goal: Continue morning mobility routine; add one 15-min solo practice session per week using the program card.

Agreed changes: Replace one isolation exercise with RDL technique work at low load; continue row progression.

## How to Deliver the Report

**Review it together, don't just send it.** A progress report sent as a PDF without discussion loses most of its value. A 10-minute conversation at the start of the first session of a new month, where you walk through it together, creates a shared understanding of where things stand and where they're going.

**Ask before presenting.** Before presenting your observations, ask the client how they feel the month went. Their perception matters — and it may differ from what the numbers say in illuminating ways.

**Be honest.** A report that only highlights positives is a marketing document, not a progress review. If something didn't go as planned, name it and explain what you'll change.

## Common Pitfalls

**Reporting metrics that weren't agreed up front.** If you suddenly introduce a metric in month 3 that wasn't discussed at the start, clients may feel surveilled or judged. Track what was agreed, revise at review points.

**Confusing the monthly report with the check-in.** The check-in informs the monthly report — it's a data source. The report synthesises that data into a coherent picture.

**Over-claiming outcomes.** "You've lost 3 kg" may be attributable to many things. Be precise: report what was measured, when, and how, without overstating what it means.

**Not updating the plan.** A progress report that ends with "keep doing the same thing" has missed its purpose. Something should change — even if that something is a deliberate commitment to continue an approach that's working.
