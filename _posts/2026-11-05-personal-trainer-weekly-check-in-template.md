A weekly check-in keeps training relevant between sessions. Without one, trainers are flying blind: they don't know if the client slept well, tweaked something, hit a stressful week at work, or quietly stopped doing the homework. With a good one, the check-in takes the client two minutes to complete and gives the trainer enough signal to adapt programming intelligently.

The key word is *compact*. A check-in that asks twelve questions will get ignored by week three. Five focused questions get answered consistently.

---

## The Template

Send this weekly — ideally on a consistent day (Sunday evening or Monday morning tends to work well for clients who train mid-week).

---

**Weekly Check-In — [Client First Name]**
*Takes about 2 minutes. No right or wrong answers.*

**1. How did training feel this week overall?**
☐ Better than expected  ☐ About right  ☐ Harder than expected  ☐ I missed sessions — that's okay, just noting it

**2. Any soreness, tightness, or discomfort worth mentioning?**
*(Be specific if yes: where, when it appeared, scale 1–10 if helpful)*

**3. How has your energy been outside of training?**
☐ Good  ☐ Variable  ☐ Low

**4. One thing that went well this week (training or otherwise):**

**5. Anything you want to adjust, try, or talk about next session?**

---

## How to Use This Template

**Delivery method.** Send via whatever channel the client agreed to at onboarding — email, WhatsApp, an app, a Google Form. Consistency matters more than platform.

**Timing.** Send it on the same day each week so the client comes to expect it. Pair it with the training schedule: if they train Monday/Thursday, a Sunday send makes sense.

**Tone.** The check-in is not a compliance check. Question 1 explicitly normalises missed sessions — "that's okay, just noting it." This gives the client room to explain what happened without being judged.

**When to act on the data.** Use check-in responses to inform the next session, not to rewrite the entire program. Ask about the context, and agree on a practical next step instead of assuming a cause.

---

## Rules for Program Adjustments

| Signal | Appropriate response |
|---|---|
| "Harder than expected" once | Ask about sleep, stress, or context before changing load |
| "Harder than expected" two weeks running | Discuss the pattern and agree on an appropriate adjustment |
| New or concerning pain | Stop the painful activity; follow your safety protocol and seek qualified assessment when appropriate |
| "Energy: Low" pattern over 3+ weeks | Check in about sleep and life stress; if unexplained and persistent, encourage GP visit |
| Missed two or more sessions | Reach out warmly, not as a reprimand (see the retention post) |

**Do not diagnose.** A client reporting persistent fatigue or unusual pain needs a conversation and, if appropriate, a referral — not a self-designed recovery protocol.

---

## Handling Non-Responses

Some weeks clients won't reply. The rule is simple:

- **After one non-response:** No action needed. Life happens.
- **After two consecutive non-responses:** Send a brief, warm nudge: *"No pressure — just checking in. Let me know if anything's come up or if you'd like to shift anything around."*
- **After three:** Address it at the next in-person session without framing it as a failure. Ask what would make the check-in more useful for them. The format may need to change.

Never use non-response as grounds for automatic program changes. You don't know what happened.

## Completed Example

**Client:** Daniel, 29, training twice a week for strength and general fitness, week 8 of a 12-week program.

---

**Weekly Check-In — Daniel**

**1. How did training feel this week overall?**
Harder than expected

**2. Any soreness, tightness, or discomfort worth mentioning?**
No discomfort to report.

**3. How has your energy been outside of training?**
Variable

**4. One thing that went well this week:**
Hit a new deadlift personal best on Monday — 100 kg for 3 reps

**5. Anything you want to adjust or talk about next session?**
Could we review my setup for the overhead press? I'm not sure where to position the weights.

---

## Sample Coach Reply

> Hi Daniel — well done on that deadlift, that's a significant milestone and reflects the consistency you've put in.
>
> Let’s review the overhead press setup together next session. You mentioned that your energy varied, so we can agree on a manageable session when we meet.
>
> See you Monday.

## Common Pitfalls

**Making check-ins feel like homework.** If the tone is evaluative ("Did you do your sessions?"), clients feel judged. Neutral, curious language gets better responses.

**Ignoring the replies.** If a client fills in the check-in and the trainer never references it, they stop bothering. Show you read it.

**Over-responding to a single bad week.** One hard week doesn't mean the program is wrong. Stable patterns over two to three weeks carry real signal; single data points usually don't.

**Using check-in data to prescribe nutrition or medical treatment.** If a client reports low energy, you can ask about sleep and suggest they see their doctor if it persists. You cannot recommend supplements, diagnose iron deficiency, or design a diet plan.
