A smooth onboarding process sets the tone for the entire client relationship. Done well, it clarifies expectations, makes the first weeks less confusing, and helps you plan appropriate sessions. Done poorly, it creates confusion, scope creep, and early dropout.

This checklist covers the full arc: from the moment someone reaches out to the end of their first month. Use it as a master reference and adapt it to your setting.

---

## The Onboarding Timeline

### Stage 1 — Enquiry (Day 0)

- [ ] Respond within 24 hours
- [ ] Clarify the client's primary goal in one sentence
- [ ] Confirm your availability matches their schedule
- [ ] Send a brief introduction: who you are, what you offer, pricing/packages
- [ ] Book a free consultation call or in-person meeting

### Stage 2 — Consultation (Days 1–3)

- [ ] Conduct a structured consultation (20–30 min; see the consultation questions post for a full agenda)
- [ ] Explain what working together involves and what it does not (scope of practice)
- [ ] Confirm the client understands you are not a dietitian, physiotherapist, or medical provider
- [ ] Identify any need for GP/physician clearance before beginning exercise (use the current PAR-Q+ or your facility's required screening tool — do not improvise screening questions)
- [ ] Answer the client's questions honestly

> **On health screening:** The PAR-Q+ is a validated pre-exercise readiness questionnaire. If a client answers "Yes" to certain questions, the form's own instructions specify follow-up steps before exercise begins. Follow those instructions exactly. Do not attempt to interpret or override them. Find a PAR-Q+ and other forms in [NASM’s assessment resources](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/assessment-information).

### Stage 3 — Admin & Agreements (Days 3–7)

- [ ] Send and collect signed service agreement / contract
- [ ] Send and collect completed intake form (goals, training history, schedule, preferences)
- [ ] Collect signed informed consent (including photography if relevant)
- [ ] Confirm preferred communication channel and response time expectations
- [ ] Agree on a cancellation and rescheduling policy in writing
- [ ] Confirm payment method and schedule
- [ ] Store all documents securely and in compliance with applicable data protection law

### Stage 4 — First Session (Week 1)

- [ ] Conduct a movement or fitness assessment appropriate to the client's goals and cleared status
- [ ] Keep the first session at moderate intensity — prioritise observation over pushing limits
- [ ] Establish baseline measurements the client has agreed to track
- [ ] Explain how sessions will be structured going forward
- [ ] Invite feedback at the end of the session

### Stage 5 — Early Weeks (Weeks 2–4)

- [ ] Introduce a weekly check-in method (see the check-in template post)
- [ ] Review and confirm program direction after week 2
- [ ] Acknowledge early wins explicitly, however small
- [ ] Flag any concerning symptoms or responses to exercise and refer appropriately
- [ ] At the end of week 4, schedule a brief review conversation

## Worked Example

**Client:** Priya, 34, enquired via Instagram about training twice a week to improve general fitness.

- **Day 0:** Trainer replied within 2 hours, confirmed 6 pm slots were available, sent a short intro message.
- **Day 2:** 25-minute call. Priya's main goal: feel less tired climbing stairs and play with her kids without aching. Trainer explained scope; administered the PAR-Q+. They followed the screening tool’s instructions and the facility’s process before planning the first session.
- **Day 4:** Service agreement and intake form sent. Both returned signed the same day. Payment set up by direct transfer.
- **Day 7:** First session. Bodyweight movement screen, light resistance work. Finished with 5 minutes of feedback. Priya said the warm-up pace felt right.
- **Week 2:** First check-in message sent Sunday. Priya said Thursday sessions felt rushed. Together they agreed on a shorter session format for busy weeks.
- **Week 4:** Brief review call. Priya confirmed she was sleeping better and felt more confident. Program adjusted to increase challenge.

## Common Pitfalls

**Skipping the consultation.** Jumping straight to a first session means you train a stranger. The consultation is where trust and understanding begin.

**Vague cancellation policy.** "Let me know if you can't make it" is not a policy. Write it down and get it signed.

**Collecting more health data than you need.** More is not safer. Collect what you will actually use, and store it securely. Unnecessary data is a liability.

**Ignoring the PAR-Q+ follow-up steps.** The screening tool gives you explicit next steps if a client flags a concern. Follow them — do not decide on your own that a flagged answer is "probably fine."

**No week-4 review.** A structured check-in gives both of you a chance to discuss what is working and what needs to change.
