The consultation is not a sales pitch, and it is not a health screening. It is a conversation designed to answer one question on both sides: *Is this a good fit, and if so, how do we begin?*

Trainers who conflate the consultation with a medical intake end up either collecting health data they are not qualified to act on, or unintentionally giving clients the impression that answering all the questions means they have been cleared to train. Neither is accurate or safe. The consultation is about goals, context, and expectations. Health screening is a separate, validated step that follows the official PAR-Q+ or your facility's required process.

---

## What a Consultation Is (and Isn't)

| A consultation is… | A consultation is not… |
|---|---|
| A two-way conversation about goals and fit | A substitute for pre-exercise health screening |
| A chance to explain your scope of practice | An opportunity to diagnose or assess medical conditions |
| A way to understand the client's life and schedule | A full fitness assessment |
| A professional first impression | A commitment to sign up |

---

## 20-Minute Agenda

**Minutes 0–2: Welcome and framing**
Set a relaxed, professional tone. Explain that this conversation helps you both figure out if working together makes sense and what that would look like.

**Minutes 2–8: The client's goal and context**
This is mostly listening. Use open questions, follow up, and don't rush to solutions.

**Minutes 8–13: Practical fit and logistics**
Schedule, commitment, setting, and expectations.

**Minutes 13–17: Your approach and scope**
What you do, what you don't do, what working together looks like day to day.

**Minutes 17–20: Questions and next steps**
Invite their questions. Agree on a clear next action before ending.

## Consultation Questions

### Opening

- "Before I ask you anything — what made you decide to reach out now?"
- "Is there anything specific you are hoping to get from this conversation today?"

### Goals and Motivation

- "What is the one outcome that would make you feel like training has been worthwhile?"
- "How long have you been thinking about this goal?"
- "Is there a specific date, event, or moment that makes this feel urgent or important?"
- "What does a good week of movement look like for you at the moment?"

### Background and Experience

- "Have you trained with a coach or trainer before? What did you take away from that?"
- "What kinds of movement have you enjoyed in the past — even if it's been a while?"
- "Are there any types of training you have tried and actively disliked?"

### Life Context and Barriers

- "What tends to get in the way when you try to build an exercise routine?"
- "How would you describe your energy levels day to day at the moment?"
- "Do you have a gym or equipment at home, or would sessions be your main access to training?"

### Expectations and Practicalities

- "How many sessions per week feel realistic for you right now — not ideal, realistic?"
- "Are there days or times that are completely unavailable for you?"
- "How do you prefer to communicate between sessions — messages, emails, app?"
- "What would make you feel like your trainer really understood you?"

### Scope and Boundaries

- "I want to make sure you know what I can help with and where I would point you elsewhere — are you aware of the difference between personal training and working with a physiotherapist or dietitian?"

## Sample Exchange (Fictional)

**Trainer:** "What made you decide to reach out now?"

**Client (Amara):** "Honestly, my doctor mentioned at a checkup that I should be more active. But I have no idea where to start — I've never really exercised."

**Trainer:** "That sounds like a meaningful moment. When you imagine being more active and feeling the benefit of it, what does that look like for you?"

**Amara:** "I think just having more stamina. I get out of breath doing ordinary things and I don't want to feel like that anymore."

**Trainer:** "That's a really clear goal. Before we get into logistics, I want to mention that since your doctor raised this, it would be worth having them confirm there are no specific conditions they want us to work around — I use a standard screening form that would cover that. Does that make sense to you?"

**Amara:** "Yes, that makes sense. I don't think there's anything serious but I'm happy to check."

**Trainer:** "Great. Now — what days and times tend to work for you?"

---

*Notice what the trainer did:* They did not ask Amara to describe her medical history. They acknowledged the GP recommendation and flagged that a standard screening step would follow. They moved quickly back to practical questions. This is the right balance.

## Listening Over Asking

The questions above are prompts, not an interrogation. After each answer:

- Pause for two seconds before responding — clients often add important detail if you don't rush in.
- Reflect back what you heard: "So if I understand you right, the main driver is stamina in daily life, not a specific event?"
- Ask one follow-up before moving on rather than jumping to the next item on the list.

Clients remember how they felt in the consultation far longer than what was said. A trainer who listens carefully in the first meeting signals what working together will be like.

## Common Pitfalls

**Turning it into a pitch.** If you spend more than three minutes explaining your qualifications and packages before asking about the client, you have lost the room.

**Premature programming.** Saying "I'd have you doing X three days a week" before you have full information sounds confident but commits you to a plan you may have to walk back.

**Skipping the scope conversation.** Clients sometimes arrive expecting nutrition plans, injury rehabilitation, or psychological support. Clarifying scope in the consultation prevents disappointment and boundary violations later.

**Using the consultation to collect screening data.** If you ask health history questions in the consultation, you may create a false sense that the client has been medically assessed. Use the official screening tool separately, at the right stage, and follow its instructions.
