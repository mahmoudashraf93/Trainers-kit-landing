The intake form is one of the most consequential pieces of paperwork in a personal training business. It shapes the program you write, the questions you ask in early sessions, and the way you understand a client’s preferences. A good intake form collects what you need and nothing more.

This article gives you a complete, copyable template with notes on what each section is for and how to use it.

---

## Before You Design Your Form

**Collect only what you will actually use.** Every extra field is either a privacy risk or noise you never read. A client form is not a medical history — you are a personal trainer, not a clinician.

**Keep health screening separate.** Pre-exercise health screening (such as the PAR-Q+) has its own validated process and its own instructions for what to do with the answers. Do not fold health-screening questions into your intake form or invent your own screening questions. Use an accepted tool, follow its instructions, and refer when indicated. Find a PAR-Q+ and other forms in [NASM’s assessment resources](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/assessment-information).

**Store it securely.** Client information is sensitive personal data. Use encrypted storage, password-protected files, or a reputable practice management platform. Check the privacy and record-keeping requirements that apply where you work.

## How to Use This Form

**Send it before the first session, not on arrival.** A client who has already reflected on their goals comes to the first session ready to have a productive conversation rather than filling in paperwork on a clipboard.

**Read it before every early session.** Trainers who don't refer back to the intake form often re-ask questions the client already answered — a credibility loss from day one.

**Question 7 (injuries/restrictions)** is a gateway, not a diagnosis. If a client discloses something significant, use it to decide whether additional clearance or a specialist referral is needed before training begins. Do not attempt to assess or manage medical conditions.

**Question 15 (comfort and accessibility)** is intentionally open. Clients can name an adjustment without explaining a diagnosis or disclosing personal history.

## Common Pitfalls

**Collecting nutrition details you will not use.** Your qualifications, certifying body and local rules determine your scope. Refer clients seeking individualized nutrition advice to an appropriately qualified professional.

**Storing forms in an unencrypted email thread.** Use a proper system. Limit who can access completed forms and follow your applicable response procedures if information is exposed.

**Never updating the form.** Circumstances change. Review key fields (injuries, availability, goals) every three to four months.
